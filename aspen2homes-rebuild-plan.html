01 · What "top of search" actually means here

Setting targets you can actually win, instead of chasing queries a small builder cannot.

You will never outrank Zillow, Redfin, or NewHomeSource for new homes in California. That's not the goal, and chasing it would waste the budget. For a builder doing 5–20 homes a year in Kern County, "top of search" means winning four specific, winnable contests:

02 · The three engines: SEO · AEO · GEO

Three different retrieval systems, one shared foundation. Get the foundation right and all three improve together.

SEO

Search Engine Optimization

Ranking in Google's blue links and the local map pack. Rewards: crawlability, keyword relevance, page speed, backlinks, user engagement, Google Business Profile signals.

Output: position in a list of 10 results.

AEO

Answer Engine Optimization

Being the answer rather than a link — featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes, knowledge panels, voice assistant replies, "zero-click" answers.

Output: a single quoted passage with your name attached.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimization

Being cited and recommended by LLMs — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Google AI Overviews, Meta AI. Rewards: entity clarity, third-party corroboration, extractable facts, review sentiment.

Output: inclusion in a synthesized recommendation.

The shared foundation — do these five and all three engines move

  1. Entity clarity. One unambiguous, machine-readable definition of who Aspen II Homes is: name, license, address, phone, service area, founding date. Published identically everywhere.
  2. Extractable facts. Real numbers in real sentences — square footage, prices, timelines, warranty terms, license numbers. LLMs cannot quote "unparalleled quality."
  3. Third-party corroboration. Independent sources repeating your facts. Google trusts links; LLMs trust agreement between sources. This is why BBB and directory cleanup matters as much as your own site.
  4. Answer-first structure. Every page answers a question in the first 40–60 words, then elaborates. This is what wins snippets and gets lifted into AI answers verbatim.
  5. Proof of legitimacy. Reviews, permits, licenses, years in business. All three engines use reputation as a trust gate, and it's currently your biggest gap.
Why this framing saves you money You do not build three separate strategies. You build one well-structured, fact-dense, fast, schema-marked site and one clean citation footprint — then you measure three different scoreboards. Every hour spent on schema serves all three engines simultaneously.

Platform, performance budget, and the infrastructure decisions.

Platform recommendation

WordPress, rebuilt lean

  • Keep WordPress — your team and Site Kit already live there
  • Remove Elementor. It is generating 31 stylesheets and 43 scripts on one page. Replace with native blocks + Kadence or GenerateBlocks
  • Theme: Kadence or GeneratePress (lightweight, block-native)

  • Ships near-zero JavaScript — near-perfect Core Web Vitals

05 · Schema — the single highest-leverage asset

The current site has zero structured data. This is the fastest large win available.

Schema markup is how you hand Google and every AI model a clean, unambiguous fact sheet about your business. Right now aspen2homes.com emits zero JSON-LD blocks — so crawlers are guessing from prose. Meanwhile BuildZoom is publishing schema about you that says you're based in Hawaii. Fixing this is roughly two hours of developer time.

/company-facts — the human-readable twin

A deliberately plain, unstyled page containing everything in the llms.txt as visible HTML, with dates and sources. Two jobs: it's the page AI systems can quote verbatim without misreading a design, and it's the page you point journalists, directory editors, and ChatGPT at when they need to verify something. Update it quarterly. Put a "Last verified" date on it — freshness signals matter to AI retrieval.

Honest expectation setting llms.txt is not yet honored by every major crawler — adoption is partial and evolving. It costs 30 minutes to write and is trivially cheap insurance if it becomes standard. Treat it as a low-cost bet, not a strategy. The schema and the facts page do the heavy lifting; llms.txt is a bonus.

08 · AEO execution plan

Winning featured snippets, People Also Ask, and the knowledge panel.

The answer-first formatting rule

AEO is a formatting discipline more than a content discipline. Every answer-bearing page follows this shape:

  1. The heading is the question, in the user's words: "How much does it cost to build a house in Tehachapi?" not "Pricing Considerations."
  2. The first paragraph is the answer: 40–60 words, complete on its own, containing the specific number or fact. No warm-up sentence. No "at Aspen II Homes we understand that…"
  3. Then elaborate with detail, nuance, and examples for people who want more.
  4. Then structure: comparison tables, numbered lists, and bulleted specs — all highly extractable.
  5. Then 3–5 FAQ pairs with FAQPage schema, matching visible text exactly.
✗ Current — unextractable

"At Aspen II Homes, we understand that your home is more than just a house; it's a reflection of your unique style and a sanctuary where cherished memories are made. With our expertise and dedication to quality, we are here to turn your dream home into a reality."

Zero extractable facts. No price, no timeline, no spec. No AI will ever quote this, and neither will a buyer.

✓ Rewritten — extractable

Aspen II Homes builds semi-custom homes from 1,507 to 2,444 sq ft in Kern County, typically priced between $449,000 and $590,000 depending on plan, lot and finish selections. Most builds take 7 to 10 months from signed contract to move-in.

Sixty words. Four hard facts. Quotable verbatim by a snippet, a voice assistant, or an LLM.

09 · GEO execution plan

Getting named by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and AI Overviews.

How AI assistants actually decide who to recommend

When someone asks an AI "who should build my house in Tehachapi?", the model does not run a keyword query. It synthesizes from a mix of: (1) its training data and any live retrieval it performs, (2) third-party sources it trusts — directories, review platforms, news, listicles, forums, (3) structured data it can parse cleanly, and (4) corroboration — do multiple independent sources agree? Your job is to be present and consistent across all four.

The six GEO plays, in priority order

PlayPriorityWhat to do
1. Own the citation surface HIGHEST Get included in "best builders in Tehachapi / Kern County" content — that is the single most-cited source type for local recommendation queries. Pitch the Tehachapi News, local real estate blogs, Houzz and Angi editorial, and regional "best of" roundups. Offer yourself as a quoted expert. This one play moves GEO more than everything else combined.
2. Build review volume HIGHEST AI weighs review corpus heavily as both a trust and a popularity signal. Two reviews is invisible. Target 25+ Google reviews, plus Yelp and Houzz. Recency matters — a steady trickle beats a one-time burst.
3. Publish extractable specifics HIGH Prices, sq ft, timelines, warranty terms, license numbers, community names. Every specific number is a thing an AI can say out loud. Vague marketing prose contributes nothing to GEO.
4. Entity disambiguation HIGH "Aspen" collides with McBride's Aspen II plan, Schumacher's Aspen II, Stonefield's Aspen, Aspen Builders, and Aspen Home Improvements. Always write "Aspen II Homes, a Kern County CA home builder" on first mention. Use alternateName in schema. Add a one-line disambiguator near the top of key pages.
5. Corroboration & cleanup HIGH Fix the BuildZoom Hawaii error and the wrong address. Claim every directory. Get the Chamber listing. Make BBB, CSLB, Facebook, and your site all say the same thing. AI trusts agreement between sources — contradictions make models cautious and they'll drop you.
6. Community presence MEDIUM Reddit (r/Tehachapi, r/Building Homes, r/Homebuilding), local Facebook groups, Houzz discussions, Quora. Genuinely helpful answers only — shilling gets you banned and can backfire into the training data. These threads are frequently scraped into AI answers.

The design principles, stated plainly

1 · Quiet confidence

Big type, lots of air, restrained color. Premium brands whisper. The current site shouts with exclamation marks and stock superlatives.

2 · Photography is the product

You're selling a $500K physical object. Images carry the page. Commission a proper shoot: 3 exteriors, 6 interiors, 1 drone pass. Roughly $1,500–3,000 once.

3 · Specificity over superlatives

"2,444 sq ft, 4 bed, 3 bath, $589,900" sells. "Unparalleled quality" does not. Every claim should be a number, a date, or a named source.

4 · Motion serves comprehension

Gentle scroll reveals, hover states with intent, smooth accordions. Nothing that spins, bounces, or slides in from the side for no reason.

5 · Mobile is the primary design

70%+ of home shoppers find you on a phone. Design the mobile layout first; desktop is the expansion.

6 · Trust is a design element

License number, BBB rating, years in business, written warranty — placed prominently, not buried in a footer. This is your differentiator and it's currently invisible.

12 · Content rewrite — kill the fluff

The copy is the biggest credibility leak on the site. Here's the replacement standard.

The rule: every claim must be a number, a date, or a named source

Go through the existing site and delete every sentence that could appear on any builder's website in America unchanged. "We are committed to sustainable building practices and incorporate energy-efficient features into every home" — says nothing, proves nothing, sells nothing. Replace with: "Every home is built to Title 24 energy standards with a HERS rating provided at close; typical utility costs run $X–$Y per month for a 2,000 sq ft plan."

✗ Delete this

"We use only the finest materials and work with skilled craftsmen who take pride in their craft. Every aspect of your home, from the layout to the finishes, is meticulously crafted to create a truly unique and breathtaking space."

"Building your dream home should be a seamless process, and that's why we handle all the details for you."

✓ Write this

"Standard specs on every Aspen II home: quartz countertops, soft-close shaker cabinetry, luxury vinyl plank or tile in wet areas, 9-foot ceilings, tankless water heater, and a covered rear patio. You choose finish packages in a single two-hour selections appointment, with pricing shown before anything is ordered."

"We pull the permits, coordinate all subcontractors, and schedule the required inspections. You get a written construction schedule at contract signing and a progress update every Friday."

Address the negative review head-on, on your own site

This is the most important paragraph in this document Your one detailed negative review claims a warranty item went unfixed for years. Whether or not that's fair, it's the first thing an AI or a careful buyer will surface. Waiting and hoping is losing. Get ahead of it with a real Warranty page that states exactly what's covered, for how long, what the response time is, and how to file a claim — with a named contact and a phone number. Then publicly respond to the review on BuildZoom: acknowledge it, state what your process is today, and offer to make it right. An unanswered one-star with no counter-narrative is the single most damaging thing in your entire digital footprint, and it is fixable this week.
